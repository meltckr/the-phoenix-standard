The Leadership Voice

Four voices, one standard

The identity is set at the top, converted into alignment by the front office, made visible daily by the staff, and held consistent across the organization.

The Acquisition Covenant

Ten questions before talent becomes identity

Acquisition is covenant. When the Phoenix Suns, Phoenix Mercury, or Valley Suns extend an offer, they are not merely transacting. They are making a binding statement about the character of this organization. The final question is the granite question.

  1. 01Identity fitDoes this player strengthen the culture leadership has publicly described?
  2. 02Work standardDoes this player work with visible seriousness - before, during, and after required activity?
  3. 03Care factorDoes the effort show on film, in practice, in preparation, and in adversity?
  4. 04AccountabilityIs this player a thumb pointer under pressure?
  5. 05CoachabilityWill this player accept demanding coaching and still trust the staff?
  6. 06ConnectivityDoes this player make teammates better emotionally, competitively, and tactically?
  7. 07DevelopmentIs there a clear plan for how this player improves inside the Phoenix system?
  8. 08Community fitWould fans and staff be proud that this person represents Phoenix basketball?
  9. 09Exception riskAre we making an exception - and if so, are we honest about what kind?
  10. 10Long-term effectIf the roster had five players with this person’s habits, would the culture be stronger?

Reading the signal

+ Green Flags
  • Relentless workExtra work that is consistent, self-directed, and not performative.
  • Positive attitude under stressEnergy that holds through losses, role changes, recovery, and criticism.
  • CoachabilitySeeks correction, applies it quickly, never personalizes hard coaching.
  • Competitive careEffort in defense, loose balls, transition, rebounding, and bench engagement.
  • Thumb-pointing accountabilityOwns mistakes, corrects behavior, avoids blame loops.
  • Joyful competitivenessSerious work without heaviness; energy that lifts teammates.
− Red Flags
  • Talent-first rationalization“He’s too good to pass up, even if the fit is complicated.”
  • Effort ambiguity“He’ll play harder when the games matter.”
  • Blame pattern“He just needs the right role, coach, market, teammates.”
  • Development resistance“He is who he is.”
  • Community disconnect“The production will make the rest manageable.”
  • Exception creep“This case is different.”

Signal definitions drawn from the Phoenix culture doctrine and its sources, including Suns on SI, Valley Suns, and UWM.
